How the group talk is graded. 100 points, worth 15% of the overall grade. The talk is graded in the room, on the day.

The comparison is the point. Two systems solving the same problem differently means a real design decision was made; your job is to find it and say what each system is optimizing for.

The mechanism in each system — 30 points

30Shows the real thing in both systems — source they read and can explain, a data structure, a measurement they ran, or a demonstration in the container.
23Concrete for one system, thinner for the other.
15Both systems described accurately, but only from secondary sources. Nothing they went and looked at themselves.
7Vague or wrong about how either system works.

The comparison — 35 points

35Names where the two designs diverge, what each one is optimizing for, and what each gives up to get it.
27Identifies the divergence, but not clearly what is traded away.
17Two descriptions placed side by side. The comparison is implied rather than made.
9Ten minutes on the first system, ten on the second, and no comparison at all.

Command of the material — 20 points

Includes how the team handles the discussion after the talk.

20Answers questions from the room and can defend every claim and every slide.
15Handles most questions; hesitant at the edges of the topic.
10Can restate the slides but not go beyond them.
5Cannot explain parts of their own talk.

Delivery and timing — 15 points

15Lands within 20 minutes, slides legible from the back, leaves room for discussion.
11Slightly over or under time; minor problems with the slides.
7Noticeably over or under; slides hard to read.
3Far off time, or slides that cannot be followed.
