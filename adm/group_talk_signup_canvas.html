This is the sign-up for the group talk, which is worth 15% of your grade. It is a group assignment: one member submits on behalf of the whole team, and Canvas records that submission for every member automatically. Do not submit separately — duplicate submissions from the same team will be treated as one.

Join your team under People → CSE4001 Group Talks first. Teams are 4 students. There are 32 of you, so there are 8 teams and 8 talk slots — every team gives exactly one talk, and every slot is taken. Due Thu Sep 10 (Week 4). The first talk is Thu Sep 24, so this deadline is what gives that team two weeks to prepare.

What is already decided, and what is yours

The topic and the date are fixed — each talk sits in the week whose lecture covers it, so the class already has the concept in hand when you present. What you choose is which slot you want and, more importantly, the second operating system. The second system in the table below is a suggestion. Propose a different one if you have a better comparison in mind, and say why.

Two systems solving the same problem differently is evidence that a real design decision was made. Your job is to find that decision and say what each system is optimizing for and what it gives up. Pick a second system whose source you can actually read or whose behaviour you can actually measure — not one you can only read about.

Team name: Zone Allocator
Members: Priya Raman, Daniel Castillo, Ama Boateng, Kevin Nowak

First choice: 6 (Kernel memory allocation)
Second choice: 3 (Isolation without a VM)
Third choice: 5 (Avoiding locks entirely)

Second system: illumos/SunOS slab allocator, not FreeBSD UMA.

Why this comparison:
Bonwick's slab allocator is the design Linux adopted, so comparing Linux slab
against the original is a comparison between an idea and its reimplementation
rather than between two unrelated designs. We want to show what Linux changed
when it borrowed the idea, and why -- per-CPU caching in particular.

How you will show the mechanism:
Read the struct definitions in both trees side by side. On Linux we will run
slabtop in the course container and watch a cache grow while we allocate, so
the class sees the object counts move rather than just a diagram.

Questions:
Is the illumos source close enough to the original SunOS code to be fair?
